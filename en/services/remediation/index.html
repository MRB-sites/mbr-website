Remediation

Older buildings can contain materials that are dangerous to demolish. We survey, protect the surroundings, remediate and document – so that demolition or renovation can be done safely.

What’s included

  • Survey and sampling when needed
  • Risk assessment and work plan
  • Barriers, enclosure and negative pressure
  • Protective equipment and decontamination
  • Remediation following Swedish Work Environment Authority regulations
  • Packaging and transport of hazardous waste
  • Documentation and receipts
  • Inspection and cleaning after remediation

What we do

  • 01

    Asbestos

    Asbestos is found in many buildings built or renovated before 1982, for example in pipe insulation, flooring, tile adhesive, fibre-cement sheets and ventilation ducts. We remove it using enclosures, negative pressure and the right protective equipment.

  • 02

    PCB

    Buildings built or renovated 1956–1973 may contain PCB in sealants, insulated glazing and floor compounds. We remediate and take PCB waste to approved facilities.

  • 03

    Mould

    We remove mould-damaged material and clean surfaces – and help address the cause together with you or your consultant.

  • 04

    Surveys

    Before demolition or conversion we inspect the building and take samples when needed, so you know what is there before work starts.

How it works

  1. 1

    Step 1

    Survey

    We inspect the building and take samples where needed.

  2. 2

    Step 2

    Plan

    Risk assessment and work plan. When required, the work is notified to the relevant authority.

  3. 3

  4. 4

Frequently asked questions

Can’t find the answer? Get in touch.

How is asbestos handled?

Asbestos is removed before any other work starts. The area is enclosed and put under negative pressure, staff use respiratory protection and protective clothing, and the waste is packaged and taken to an approved facility. The work follows the Swedish Work Environment Authority’s regulations, which require special training and in some cases a permit.

How do I know if my building contains asbestos or PCB?

The construction year gives an indication: asbestos mainly occurs in buildings built or renovated before 1982, PCB in buildings built or renovated 1956–1973. A survey with sampling gives a definite answer.

Who is responsible for surveying hazardous materials?

The developer – usually the property owner – is responsible for identifying hazardous materials before demolition. We can carry out the survey for you.

Do I have to move out during remediation?

It depends on where and how much needs to be remediated. You can often stay if the work area is sealed off. We go through this at the site visit.

Can I use the ROT tax deduction?

Remediation done as part of renovating your home may in some cases qualify for the ROT deduction on labour costs. The rules change from time to time, so check with the Swedish Tax Agency. We itemise the labour cost on the invoice.
